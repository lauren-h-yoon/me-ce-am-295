**Week 9 Lecture Outline: Evaluation, Reliability, and Deployment**

Format: single Tuesday lecture this week (Thanksgiving), 1:00–2:30 p.m. (90 minutes) + 5-hour at-home laboratory. Target Audience: Mechanical Engineering and Materials Science graduate students and senior undergraduates. Prerequisites: Weeks 1–8; a working capstone prototype.

*Delivery (Fall 2026): Tuesday Nov 24 only; no Thursday session (Institute holiday). Quiz 9 is taken online. No deadlines Nov 25–29.*

0:00 - 0:05 \| Week 9 Objectives — from demo to evidence of reliability

0:05 - 0:25 \| Hallucination in Safety-Critical Contexts — fabricated standards provisions, impossible properties, silent arithmetic failures; neuro-symbolic checking and Reviewer agents

0:25 - 0:45 \| Uncertainty Quantification and Calibration — token-level probabilities, consistency-based UQ, calibration as an engineering requirement

0:45 - 1:05 \| Behavioral Testing of Stochastic Agent Systems — consistency, accuracy, process adherence, degradation; 20+ runs per critical case; the surface-correctness trap

1:05 - 1:20 \| Data Control and the Production-Readiness Checklist — protected data channels; the five-part deployment checklist

1:20 - 1:30 \| Lab 9 Briefing — reliability audit of your own capstone system (deliverable: 1–2 page reliability report)

Core readings: Cemri et al., "Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?" (MAST), NeurIPS 2025 (Ref_25); supplementary: tau-bench (Ref_33), MCP specification (Ref_19).
