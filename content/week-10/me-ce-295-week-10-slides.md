PART I

AI and the Reproducibility Crisis

Ill-informed use of machine learning is driving a deluge of unreliable research.

## Slide 3

PART II

The Regulatory Landscape

Responsible charge (NSPE)

“AI use does not change a licensed engineer’s legal responsibilities. Engineers must still exercise responsible charge and validate all outputs — whether generated by a junior engineer or an AI system.”

EU AI Act

AI systems used in the management and operation of critical infrastructure are classified high-risk: rigorous human oversight, detailed logging, and certification are required.

In research settings

The same principles appear as research integrity, export control on models and data, and the physical safety of autonomous laboratories (Week 8). AI agents are tools, not licensed professionals.

## Slide 4

PART III

Authorship and Intellectual Property

Patents

Thaler v. Vidal (Fed. Cir. 2022; cert. denied 2023): the Patent Act requires inventors to be human beings. An AI cannot be a named inventor.

Copyright

The US Copyright Office requires sufficient human authorship to register AI-assisted works. Purely machine-generated content is not registrable.

Journal authorship

Nature, Science, and others: LLMs cannot be credited as authors — they cannot take legal or ethical responsibility for the work.

Why this course requires an AI Use Declaration: transparency about tool use, with responsibility remaining human — the same principle that governs patents, copyright, and authorship.
